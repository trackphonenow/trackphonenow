Can a Phone Be Tracked with IMEI if the SIM Card Is Removed?

One of the first actions thieves take after taking a smartphone is to pop out the SIM card tray. The widespread belief is that removing the SIM makes the device completely invisible. But does ejecting a SIM card actually defeat cellular tracking? Here is what happens inside the cellular radio silicon.

EXECUTIVE ANSWER

Yes, a Phone Can Still Be Tracked After the SIM Card Is Removed

The SIM card is just a plastic authentication chip; the cellular radio antenna is built permanently into your phone. Under international 3GPP telecom standards, every smartphone must be capable of completing emergency calls without a SIM card. To do this, the phone’s baseband modem constantly maintains radio contact with local cell towers, transmitting its 15-digit hardware IMEI.

Emergency Radio Channel: Mobile modems listen and broadcast emergency beacons without any SIM.
Permanent Silicon IMEI: The 15-digit transceiver serial is burned into modem chips and never changes.
Instant SIM Swap Bind: If a thief inserts another SIM, carrier networks immediately record the pairing.
Cell Sector Lock: Tower antennas calculate signal timing advance down to a doorstep radius.

1. Why Removing the SIM Fails to Stop Radio Telemetry

To understand why ejecting the SIM card does not protect a thief from detection, consider how cellular networks operate. The SIM card merely holds your carrier subscriber key (the IMSI). The actual radio transmitter and receiver belong to the Baseband Processor (SoC) inside the phone.

International telecommunications mandates require all cellular devices to connect to any available mobile tower regardless of carrier affiliation or SIM presence. When powered on:

  1. The baseband modem performs a frequency scan across 700MHz to 3.5GHz bands.
  2. It negotiates a physical radio channel with the strongest local Base Transceiver Station (BTS).
  3. The tower queries: "Identify device transceiver." The handset replies with its permanent 15-digit IMEI.
  4. The cell tower registers the handshake and records the device's signal strength and physical distance (Timing Advance).

2. What Happens When the Thief Inserts Their Own SIM?

Thieves rarely keep a phone without a SIM card forever. Eventually, they insert a replacement SIM card—whether their personal card or a prepaid burner SIM—to test if the device works.

The instant this happens, the mobile carrier executes an IMSI-IMEI Binding Event:

  • The network links the thief’s new telephone number directly to your hardware IMEI in the central Equipment Identity Register (EIR).
  • The carrier logs the exact serving cell sector and coordinates.
  • Platforms like TrackPhoneNow query these active carrier gateways to refresh real-time coordinates.

Learn more about this mechanism in our detailed guide on what happens when a thief changes your SIM card.

3. Hardware IMEI vs. Software Factory Resets

Even if the thief attempts to wipe the handset through recovery mode, the operating system wipe only cleans flash user storage (photos, apps, and Google/Apple logins). The radio modem firmware and its burned-in IMEI survive completely intact. Read our technical study on can a phone be tracked after a factory reset.

Forensic Fact: Removing the SIM card does not make a phone invisible. The baseband radio transceiver continues to register with local mobile towers whenever the device has power.
