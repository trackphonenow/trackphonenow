1. Why Google Find My Device Fails After a Factory Reset

Google's consumer location service depends completely on Google Play Services and active OAuth tokens stored in the Android /data user partition.

When a thief performs a recovery wipe (Volume Up + Power Button > Wipe data/factory reset):

  • The /data and /cache partitions are formatted to zero.
  • Your Google account login credentials, synchronization tokens, and Play Services background daemons are destroyed.
  • When you open Google Find My Device, it shows: "Device not reachable" or "Last seen X days ago."

Crucial Takeaway: Google's software is dead, but the physical smartphone hardware is very much alive.

2. The Alternative: Carrier Equipment Identity Register (EIR) & IMEI Telemetry

Every smartphone built contains two distinct computers inside one chassis:

  1. Application Processor (AP): Runs Android, apps, games, and Google services. This is what gets wiped during a reset.
  2. Baseband Processor (BP): An isolated hardware modem chip running its own real-time operating system (RTOS). This controls the cellular antennas and contains the permanent 15-digit IMEI number.

A factory reset never touches the baseband processor. The second the phone powers on at the startup wizard, the baseband modem automatically broadcasts an IMSI/IMEI Attach Message to the nearest mobile cell tower.

By routing through telecom SS7 signaling gateways and querying carrier EIR registers, the handset's active cellular connection and timing advance distance can be calculated without needing any Google account.
